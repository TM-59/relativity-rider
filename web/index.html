02 / BEYOND THE ROCKET

Orbit a black hole.

General relativity around a mass M (G = c = 1: lengths and times in units of M; for the Sun M = 1.48 km). The same C, compiled to WebAssembly, integrates the geodesic with a fourth-order symplectic step. The orbit turns a little every lap, light bends and can be captured, and no circular orbit survives inside 6 M.

03 / THE VIEW FROM THE WINDOW

Look out at near light speed.

The ship moves at β = v / c past stars that are at rest. Light from each star reaches the moving ship from further ahead (aberration), shifted in frequency by the Doppler factor D, and the stars crowd toward the direction of motion: half of all of them sit inside a cone of half-angle arccos β. The same C, compiled to WebAssembly, computes every star's direction, D, black-body colour and visible-band brightness; JavaScript only draws the dots.

FIELD NOTES

Same distance, less time on board.

At a constant 1 g the rocket reaches the half-way point at 0.95 c. The full flip-and-burn trip takes 3.53 years on Vela's clock and 5.82 years on Oren's. The worldline never leaves the light cone: nothing here goes faster than light.

The same model as the terminal game

Each step advances the ship clock by 0.0044 yr with the exact closed form of hyperbolic motion (units c = 1, years and light-years), in C compiled to WebAssembly. JavaScript only draws and reads inputs. The paintings and the two foxes are illustrations; their markers sit on computed events.
